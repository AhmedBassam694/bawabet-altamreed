👂 Ear Anatomy

  • Ear = Organ of Hearing.
  • Divided into 3 Parts:
    • External Ear.
    • Middle Ear.
    • Inner Ear.
  • External Ear:
    • Pinna (Auricle).
    • External Auditory Canal.
    • Tympanic Membrane (Eardrum).
  • Middle Ear:
    • Air-filled cavity.
    • Contains 3 Ossicles:
      • Malleus.
      • Incus.
      • Stapes.
    • Eustachian Tube equalizes pressure.
  • Inner Ear:
    • Cochlea.
    • Auditory Nerve.
    • Hair Cells.
💡 Remember:
External → Middle → Inner Ear.

🔊 Sense of Hearing

  1. Sound waves collected by the Pinna.
  2. Travel through External Auditory Canal.
  3. Hit Tympanic Membrane.
  4. Ossicles amplify vibration.
  5. Stapes transfers vibration to Cochlea.
  6. Hair Cells convert vibration into nerve impulses.
  7. Auditory Nerve carries impulses to Brain.
  8. Brain interprets sound.
💡 Sequence: Pinna → Canal → Tympanic Membrane → Ossicles → Cochlea → Auditory Nerve → Brain.

🟡 Ear Wax (Cerumen)

  • Secreted by Ceruminous Glands.
  • Protects ear by trapping dust.
  • Normally leaves ear naturally.
  • Excess wax may cause:
    • Blocked Ear.
    • Temporary Hearing Loss.
  • Removal methods:
    • Syringing.
    • Suction.
    • Special Instrument.
    • Wax-softening drops before irrigation.
⚠️ Never clean the ear with sharp objects.

🔥 Otitis Externa (Swimmer's Ear)

  • Inflammation of External Auditory Canal.
  • Common causes:
    • Bacterial infection.
    • Fungal infection.
    • Trauma from cleaning the ear.
    • Water trapped inside ear.
  • Symptoms:
    • Ear pain.
    • Itching.
    • Redness and swelling.
    • Discharge from ear.
    • Temporary hearing loss.
  • Treatment:
    • Keep ear dry.
    • Antibiotic ear drops.
    • Analgesics for pain relief.
    • Clean ear canal by doctor.
💡 Remember:
Otitis Externa = Infection of External Ear Canal.

👂 Otitis Media

  • Inflammation of Middle Ear.
  • Common especially in children.
  • Usually occurs after upper respiratory infection.
  • Caused by bacteria or viruses.

Symptoms:

  • Severe ear pain.
  • Fever.
  • Irritability in children.
  • Hearing difficulty.
  • Fluid behind tympanic membrane.

Treatment:

  • Antibiotics if bacterial infection.
  • Analgesics.
  • Antipyretics.
  • Myringotomy may be done if fluid accumulates.
💡 Difference:
External Ear → Otitis Externa.
Middle Ear → Otitis Media.

⚠️ Foreign Bodies in Ear

  • Common in children.
  • Examples:
    • Beads.
    • Seeds.
    • Small toys.
    • Insects.
  • Symptoms:
    • Pain.
    • Discharge.
    • Hearing loss.
    • Feeling of something inside ear.
  • Management:
    • Remove carefully by specialist.
    • Do not use sharp objects.
⚠️ Never attempt forceful removal.

😴 Snoring

  • Sound produced during sleep due to vibration of airway tissues.
  • Causes:
    • Nasal obstruction.
    • Enlarged tonsils.
    • Adenoids.
    • Obesity.
  • May be associated with Obstructive Sleep Apnea.
  • Treatment:
    • Treat nasal obstruction.
    • Weight reduction.
    • Surgery for enlarged tonsils or adenoids if needed.

🩸 Epistaxis (Nose Bleeding)

  • Bleeding from the nasal cavity.
  • Common causes:
    • Nose injury.
    • Dry air.
    • Nose picking.
    • Hypertension.
    • Blood disorders.

    • Types:

  • Anterior Epistaxis:
    • Most common type.
    • Occurs in front part of nose.
  • Posterior Epistaxis:
    • Less common.
    • More severe bleeding.

First Aid:

  • Patient sits upright and leans forward.
  • Apply pressure on soft part of nose.
  • Cold compress may be applied.
  • Medical treatment if bleeding continues.
💡 Remember:
Never tilt the head backward during nose bleeding.

🤧 Sinusitis

  • Inflammation of the paranasal sinuses.
  • Causes:
    • Viral infection.
    • Bacterial infection.
    • Allergy.

    • Symptoms:

  • Facial pain or pressure.
  • Nasal congestion.
  • Thick nasal discharge.
  • Headache.
  • Fever.

Treatment:

  • Rest and fluids.
  • Nasal saline wash.
  • Analgesics.
  • Antibiotics if bacterial infection.

🔥 Tonsillitis

  • Inflammation of the tonsils.
  • Caused by viruses or bacteria.

Symptoms:

  • Sore throat.
  • Difficulty swallowing.
  • Fever.
  • Enlarged red tonsils.
  • White patches on tonsils.
  • Swollen neck lymph nodes.

Treatment:

  • Rest and fluids.
  • Antipyretics and analgesics.
  • Antibiotics for bacterial cases.
  • Tonsillectomy in recurrent severe cases.
💡 Tonsillitis = Inflammation of Tonsils.

👃 Adenoids

  • Adenoids are lymphoid tissue located behind the nose.
  • Enlargement is common in children.

Symptoms of Enlarged Adenoids:

  • Mouth breathing.
  • Nasal obstruction.
  • Snoring.
  • Sleep problems.
  • Recurrent ear infections.

Treatment:

  • Medical treatment for mild cases.
  • Adenoidectomy if severe obstruction occurs.

🗣️ Laryngeal Obstruction

  • Blockage of the larynx that affects breathing.
  • May cause airway obstruction and difficulty breathing.

    • Causes:

  • Foreign bodies.
  • Swelling due to infection.
  • Tumors.
  • Trauma.

Symptoms:

  • Difficulty breathing (Dyspnea).
  • Noisy breathing (Stridor).
  • Change in voice.
  • Cough.
  • Blue discoloration of lips in severe cases.

Management:

  • Maintain airway.
  • Remove obstruction if possible.
  • Oxygen therapy.
  • Emergency tracheostomy if airway cannot be maintained.
⚠️ Laryngeal obstruction is a medical emergency.

🫁 Tracheostomy

  • Surgical opening created in the trachea to provide an airway.
  • A tracheostomy tube is inserted through the opening.

Indications:

  • Upper airway obstruction.
  • Long-term ventilation support.
  • Severe respiratory problems.
  • Removal of airway secretions.

Types:

  • Temporary Tracheostomy.
  • Permanent Tracheostomy.

Nursing Care:

  • Maintain airway patency.
  • Check tube position.
  • Suction secretions when needed.
  • Provide humidified oxygen.
  • Clean around stoma site.
  • Observe for infection signs.
💡 Remember:
Tracheostomy = Artificial opening in the trachea to help breathing.

📌 Chapter Summary

  • Ear has 3 parts: External, Middle, Inner.
  • Hearing pathway: Pinna → Canal → Tympanic Membrane → Ossicles → Cochlea → Auditory Nerve → Brain.
  • Otitis Externa affects external ear canal.
  • Otitis Media affects middle ear.
  • Epistaxis means nose bleeding.
  • Sinusitis is inflammation of paranasal sinuses.
  • Tonsillitis affects tonsils.
  • Adenoids enlargement may cause snoring and nasal obstruction.
  • Tracheostomy provides an artificial airway.
