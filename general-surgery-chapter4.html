Postoperative Complications

Postoperative Complications

Surgical complications result from a disease the patient suffered from before the operation, or from the disease for which the patient underwent the operation, or from the effect of the surgery itself in addition to complications of anesthesia.

Complications of Surgical Operations

  • Complications of anesthesia
  • Complications of fluid transfusion
  • Complications of the wound
  • Respiratory complications
  • Complications of the heart and veins
  • Digestive system complications
  • Liver complications
  • Kidney and urinary system complications
  • Elevated temperature after surgery

Complications of Anesthesia

First: Complications of General Anesthesia

1- Malignant hyperthermia: May appear during surgery or within 24 hours of the operation.

Complications include:

  • Increased pulse and pressure
  • Elevated temperature
  • Muscle rigidity
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Vocal cord spasm
  • Urinary retention
  • Decreased temperature
  • Injury of some nerves due to wrong positioning of the patient on the operating table

Secondly: Complications of Spinal Anesthesia

  • Drop in blood pressure.
  • Respiratory disturbance due to high level of spinal anesthesia.
  • Headache especially in young people.
  • Urinary retention.
  • Meningitis (very rare condition).
  • Nerve injury (very rare).

Complications of Solutions Transfusion

Local Complications

1- Leakage of fluids under the skin and around the cannula:

Symptoms:

  • Swelling around the cannula.
  • Pain.
  • Stopping of the drip.

Treatment:

  • Moving the cannula to another site.
  • Using ointment to help absorption of fluids.

2- Phlebitis:

Symptoms:

  • Swelling.
  • Bluish discoloration.
  • Pain with warmth in the vein.

Treatment:

  • Stopping the drip.
  • Applying anti-inflammatory ointment.
  • Warm compresses.

3- Microbial inflammation at cannula or injection site.

General Complications

1- Pyrogenic Reaction:

A sudden rise in temperature after injecting solutions due to impurities in the drip.

Symptoms:

  • Sudden rise in temperature.
  • Chills.
  • Shivering.

Treatment:

  • Stopping the drip.
  • Anti-allergy drugs like Apil or Decadron.

2- Air Embolism:

A rare condition that may occur with insertion of a central venous catheter.

Prevention:

  • Prevent introducing air into catheter or solution bottles.
  • Clamp the catheter when changing solutions.

Symptoms:

  • Shock symptoms.
  • Cyanosis.

Treatment:

  • Lower the head of the bed.
  • Place patient on left side.
  • Give oxygen.

3- Pulmonary Embolism:

Occurs when a clot travels to lung vessels.

Symptoms:

  • Rapid pulse.
  • Rapid breathing.
  • High pressure.
  • Cyanosis.

Treatment:

  • Transfer patient to ICU.
  • Give oxygen.
  • Give anticoagulants like Heparin.

4- Increased Amount of Fluids in the Body:

Occurs when large amounts of solutions are injected too quickly, especially in cardiac patients, renal patients, children, and elderly patients.

Symptoms:

  • Difficulty breathing in lying position.
  • Cyanosis.
  • Distension of neck veins.
  • High blood pressure.
  • Persistent cough.
  • Edema.

Treatment:

  • Stop the solutions.
  • Place the patient in sitting position with head elevated 45 degrees.
  • Give diuretics like Lasix.

Complications of the Wound

1- Hematoma:

Resulting from bleeding due to surgical problems or patient's tendency to bleeding.

2- Seroma:

Occurs after operations like mastectomy or hernia repair due to cutting of lymphatic vessels under the skin.

It appears as swelling at the operation site.

Treatment:

  • Pressure dressing for mild swelling.
  • Drugs that help absorption of collected fluids like Alpha Chymotrypsin.
  • Aspiration of fluid using syringe if swelling increases.

3- Wound Dehiscence:

Results from improper suturing of muscles or after operations for peritonitis and acute pancreatitis.

The wound first secretes serous fluid then becomes opened.

Treatment:

  • Urgent exploration to detect internal causes.
  • Resuturing of the wound.

Respiratory Complications

These complications occur most frequently after chest, heart, and upper abdominal surgeries.

Atelectasis:

Results from bronchial obstruction and weak breathing.

Symptoms:

  • Rise in temperature.
  • Rapid breathing.
  • Rapid pulse.

Prevention:

  • Early mobilization after surgery.
  • Breathing exercises.

Treatment:

  • Bronchodilators.
  • Expectorants.

Bronchitis:

Results from aspiration of fluids into the lungs.

Symptoms:

  • Elevated temperature.
  • Difficulty breathing.
  • Low blood oxygen level.

Prevention:

  • Fasting before surgery.
  • NG tube insertion before emergency surgery.
  • Giving Zantac.

Treatment:

  • Tracheal suction.
  • Hydrocortisone.
  • Antibiotics.

Pneumonia:

Results from infection of the lung by microbes.

Diagnosis:

  • Plain X-ray.
  • CT scan.

Prevention:

  • Same prevention methods of lung aspiration.

Treatment:

  • Antibiotics.
  • Rest.
  • Antipyretics.
  • Expectorants.
  • Ventilator in case of lung failure and low oxygen level.

Cardiac and Venous Complications After Surgical Operations

1- Cardiac Arrhythmias:

Includes impaired heart function, coronary insufficiency, and myocardial infarction.

It may occur in cardiac patients after surgery, especially after heart attacks or undiagnosed heart defects.

2- Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

Deep vein thrombosis occurs in leg veins after surgery due to blood stasis caused by prolonged immobility.

It occurs more frequently in elderly patients and obese patients.

Prevention of DVT:

  • Anticoagulant drugs before and after surgery such as LMWH (Clexane - Enoxaparin).
  • Avoid dehydration by giving suitable fluids.
  • Encourage patient movement after surgery.

Symptoms of DVT:

  • Swelling of one leg compared with the other.
  • Leg pain especially during movement.
  • Slight rise in temperature.

Diagnosis:

  • Duplex scan (Ultrasound Doppler).

Treatment of DVT:

  • Give Heparin by continuous IV infusion using control pump.
  • Follow PTT test daily to adjust dose.
  • Bed rest until acute symptoms disappear.
  • Increase oral fluids with IV fluids.

3- Pulmonary Embolism:

It is the most dangerous complication of deep vein thrombosis. It occurs when a part of the leg clot travels to the pulmonary artery causing obstruction.

Symptoms:

  • Sudden difficulty breathing.
  • Cyanosis (bluish discoloration).
  • Rapid and weak pulse.
  • Drop in blood pressure.

Diagnosis:

  • ECG changes.
  • Decrease in oxygen percentage and increase in carbon dioxide in blood gases.

Treatment:

  • Give oxygen to increase blood oxygen level.
  • Give fluids to raise blood pressure.
  • Continuous IV Heparin infusion.
  • Insert filter in inferior vena cava in large clots or recurrent pulmonary embolism.

Complications of the Digestive System

1- Paralytic Ileus:

Results from potassium deficiency and after operations for peritonitis and pancreatitis.

2- Gastric Dilatation:

Occurs after stomach operations without using NG tube or suction.

Symptoms:

  • Severe abdominal distension.
  • Difficulty breathing.

Treatment:

  • Insertion of NG tube.
  • Apply gastric suction.

3- Intestinal Obstruction:

Results from adhesions inside peritoneal tissue or internal hernia.

Symptoms:

  • Abdominal distension.
  • Colicky pain.
  • Increased gastric reflux through NG tube.

Diagnosis:

  • Plain upright abdominal X-ray.

Treatment:

  • Stop oral food intake.
  • Insert NG tube.
  • Give fluids.

4- Fecal Impaction:

Occurs more commonly in elderly patients and may lead to distension and paralytic ileus.

Diagnosis:

  • Digital rectal examination.

Treatment:

  • Evacuation of feces.

Liver Complications

1- Hepatitis:

May occur due to allergy to anesthesia.

2- Viral Hepatitis:

Results from blood transfusion contaminated with Hepatitis B or C viruses.

Prevention:

  • Careful blood testing before transfusion.

3- Obstructive Jaundice:

Occurs due to injury or ligation of the bile duct especially during gallbladder and stomach operations.

Kidney and Urinary Tract Complications

1- Urinary Retention:

May occur after hernia operations due to pain, after prostate operations, or in elderly men with prostate enlargement.

Prevention:

  • Treatment of prostate problems.
  • Pain relief after hernia operations.

Treatment:

  • Give analgesics.
  • Help the patient relax and try urination.
  • Insert urinary catheter under complete aseptic conditions if needed.

2- Urethritis:

Results from hospital-acquired infection after bladder catheterization.

Prevention:

  • Proper sterilization during catheter insertion.

Treatment:

  • Increase fluids.
  • Give suitable antibiotics.

Fever After Surgical Operations

Fever occurs in about 40% of patients after surgery.

Causes:

Within 48 hours after surgery:

  • Anesthesia.
  • IV solutions.
  • Blood transfusion.
  • Atelectasis.
  • The original disease.

After 48 hours:

  • Wound contamination.
  • Inflammation during operation.
  • Cannula site infection.
  • Pneumonia.
  • Urinary tract infection.

From the fifth to seventh day:

  • Wound infection.
  • Internal contamination after surgery.
  • Abscess at operation site.
  • Deep vein thrombosis.

Blood Transfusion

Complications of Blood Transfusion:

  • Complications of fluid transfusion.
  • Blood reaction due to incompatibility.
  • Disease transmission.
  • Jaundice.

Incompatibility Reaction:

It is the most dangerous complication of blood transfusion.

Causes:

  • Error in crossmatch between patient's and donor's blood.
  • Error in recording blood type on blood bag.
  • Error in recording patient or donor name.
  • Error in compatibility test.
  • Incompatibility between minor blood groups.

Symptoms of Incompatibility:

  • High temperature and chills.
  • Rapid pulse.
  • Drop in blood pressure.
  • Kidney region pain.
  • Difficulty breathing.
  • Cyanosis of lips and extremities.

Prevention:

  • Ensure correct blood typing tests.
  • Use new tools for each compatibility test.
  • Correct recording of patient and donor names.
  • Verify blood type before transfusion.
  • Start blood slowly especially the first 100 ml.
  • Observe patient during transfusion.

Treatment:

  • Stop blood transfusion immediately.
  • Give corticosteroids IV.
  • Give Mannitol IV.
  • Give calcium IV very slowly.

Disease Transmission Via Blood

Blood transfusion may transmit many diseases such as:

  • AIDS (HIV).
  • Syphilis.
  • Malaria.
  • Hepatitis B and C.

Therefore, all necessary tests must be performed before blood transfusion.

Jaundice

Jaundice may occur after blood transfusion due to infection with Hepatitis B or C viruses.

Other Causes:

  • Contaminated needles or IV sets.
  • Hemolysis due to old stored blood.
  • Hemolysis due to incompatibility reaction.

Determining Blood Group

Two drops of patient's blood are placed on a glass slide.

  • Anti-A serum is added to the first drop.
  • Anti-B serum is added to the second drop.

Results:

  • No agglutination → Blood group O.
  • Agglutination in both drops → Blood group AB.
  • Agglutination with Anti-A only → Blood group A.
  • Agglutination with Anti-B only → Blood group B.

Rh Factor

  • Rh Positive (Rh +ve): About 85%.
  • Rh Negative (Rh -ve): About 15%.

Rh factor is important during pregnancy. If an Rh-negative mother is exposed to Rh-positive blood and the fetus is Rh-positive, a reaction may occur causing fetal jaundice.
