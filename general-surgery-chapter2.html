Chapter 2 - Fluids and Electrolytes

💧 Fluids and Electrolytes

💦 Body Fluids

Water forms about 60% of body weight.

  • Plasma → 5%
  • Interstitial Fluid → 15%
  • Intracellular Fluid → 40%

⚡ Electrolytes

Electrolytes are minerals that regulate body functions.

  • Inside Cells → Potassium, Phosphate, Bicarbonate.
  • Outside Cells → Sodium, Chloride.

⚖️ Water Balance

The body maintains water balance through intake and loss.

  • Water intake.
  • Food.
  • Metabolism.
  • Loss through kidneys.
  • Loss through skin.
  • Loss through lungs.

🚰 Dehydration

Occurs due to excessive fluid loss without replacement.

Causes:
  • Severe diarrhea.
  • Repeated vomiting.
  • Intestinal obstruction.
  • During surgery.
Symptoms:
  • Thirst.
  • Dry mouth.
  • Headache.
  • Dry tongue.
  • Low urine output.
Treatment:
  • Glucose 5% IV.
  • Glucose Saline.

🟣 Potassium (K⁺)

Normal Level: 3.5 – 5.0 mEq/L

Low Potassium:
  • Vomiting.
  • Diarrhea.
  • Diuretics.
  • Insulin.
Symptoms:
  • Muscle weakness.
  • Abdominal distension.
Treatment:
  • Oral Potassium.
  • Slow IV Potassium.

🦴 Calcium (Ca²⁺)

Low Calcium:
  • Renal failure.
  • Parathyroid deficiency.
  • Diuretics.
Symptoms:
  • Tingling.
  • Muscle spasm.
  • Hypotension.
Treatment:
  • Slow IV Calcium Gluconate.

🧂 Sodium (Na⁺)

High Sodium:
  • Too much saline.
  • Severe dehydration.
Low Sodium:
  • Diuretics.
  • Diarrhea.
  • Heart failure.
  • Renal failure.
Important:

Sodium replacement must be slow to avoid brain damage.
